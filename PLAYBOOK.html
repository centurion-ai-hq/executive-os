The Playbook

Three pages. Everything you need to run your chief of staff. Keep this tab open.

You now have an assistant that remembers, that has your standing preferences in writing, and that will not send anything to a human being without asking you first.

The one rule

If you remember nothing else on this page, remember this.

Say it in your own words

You do not need to learn commands, phrasing, or prompt tricks. Talk the way you would talk to a sharp chief of staff who has been with you a year. Ramble if you want. Dictate if that is faster. It sorts what you said.

You never have to pick a skill. 27 of them are installed and every one of them listens. Something reads what you wrote before it reaches your assistant, works out which skill fits, and hands over that skill's full procedure. You say a sentence. The right machinery runs.

When you are lost, two phrases always work: what just happened and what should I do next.

What it is allowed to do on its own

  • Read anything on your computer, and search the web
  • Write, organise, and rewrite files in your work folder
  • Draft anything: emails, memos, proposals, plans, reports
  • Research, analyse, build, and check its own work

What it must ask you first, every single time

  • Send anything to another person. It drafts. You send.
  • Publish or post anything publicly
  • Spend money, or put a dollar figure in front of a named person
  • Delete anything, or overwrite something with no second copy
This never changes

It will never ask you for a password, a card number, or an ID number. If anything ever asks you to paste one into the chat, stop and say so. Nothing in this system needs one.

The five full sequences

A drill is not one action. It is a whole rehearsed sequence that runs off one sentence and hands you a single report at the end.

DrillWhat runs
pressure-testFour angles at a plan: the facts, the case against, is it too big, does the money work. One verdict.
prove-itA claim checked against primary evidence. Comes back proved, disproved, or honestly unproven.
check numbersEvery figure traced back to where it came from. Untraceable numbers get flagged, not trusted.
lesson-learnedA correction you gave, logged and turned into something that cannot happen twice.
delegate-to-agentsA researcher, a drafter and a reviewer run as separate workers, then one finished result.

The brain dump is the point

Most people try to write a tidy request. Do not. The single highest value thing you can do with this system is open your mouth and let everything out at once, out of order, mid sentence, the way it actually sits in your head.

It splits what you said into every distinct item, numbers each one so you can both refer back to it, and sorts each into what is being handled now, what is real work for later, what is waiting on someone else, what you have to decide, and what is just context worth keeping. You see that list before it starts, so you can see nothing was lost.

Why this matters

The thing you mention once, in passing, halfway through a sentence, is usually the thing that costs you when it is forgotten. That is the item this is built to catch.

When you correct it

Say what was wrong, plainly. It writes the correction to a permanent log and reads that log at the start of every future session. You should not have to give the same correction twice. If you ever do, tell it, and that becomes a correction of its own.

When you decide something

The moment you rule on something, name a price, or set a date, it gets written down. You can ask what have we decided at any point and get the whole list back with dates.

Five files. You never have to touch them, but they are yours and they are readable.

To change how it works with you, say update my handbook and tell it what you want different. To save your place before you step away, say I have to go, save my place.

The habit that makes this compound

Once a week, when you catch yourself asking for the same kind of thing a third time, say turn this into a skill. It writes a permanent one phrase shortcut for that exact task, tests it in front of you, and tells you the phrase that now triggers it.

That is how this stops being a tool you were given and starts being a system you own.

Your next move

Open the cheat sheet, pick one skill you have not used, and use it on something real today. Not a test. Real work. That is the only way the first week sticks.
