# Objection Prep

Names the five objections most likely to come up in a specific deal, ranked by how much damage each one does, with a real answer for each.

## When this fires

• The user names an upcoming meeting or call where pushback is expected.
• The user says "prep me for objections", "what will they push back on", "help me get ready for this pitch".
• A deal is at a stage, proposal sent or final decision, where resistance is the main remaining barrier.

## What you do

1. Get the deal context: who the buyer is, what has been discussed so far, and what is known about their hesitations, from notes, prior calls, or the user's own read of the room.
2. List the five objections most likely given that context. Ground each one in something specific about this deal (a budget cycle, a competitor mentioned, a past bad experience they referenced), not a generic list of common sales objections.
3. Rank the five by how much damage each does if left unanswered: which one actually kills the deal versus which one is a formality.
4. For each objection, write a real answer in the user's own voice, specific to their offer, not a scripted sales-training line. Base it on facts the user actually has (a number, a case, a guarantee) rather than a persuasive-sounding assertion with nothing behind it.
5. Identify the one objection the user cannot yet answer well. Say plainly what is missing (a reference, a number, a policy answer) and what the user needs to go get before the meeting.
6. Keep every answer short enough to say out loud in under 20 seconds; this is prep for a live conversation, not a document to read from.

## Output

A ranked list, most damaging first:
• **Objection:** stated plainly, as the buyer would actually say it.
• **Damage if unanswered:** why this one matters more or less than the others.
• **Your answer:** the real response, in the user's voice, under 20 seconds spoken.
Closing line: the one objection with no good answer yet, and the specific thing to go find before the meeting.

## Rules

• Every answer must be something the user could actually say and defend if pushed further. No invented statistics, fake case studies, or unconfirmed claims.
• Naming an unanswerable objection is the point of this skill, not a failure of it. Never paper over a real gap with a vague answer.
• This skill prepares talking points only. It does not draft or send anything to the prospect.
