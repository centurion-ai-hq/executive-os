# Volunteer Programme

Builds out the volunteer side of a programme: the role, how someone gets started, when they show
up, and how they get thanked, so people who give their time actually come back.

## When this fires
• The user needs a new volunteer role filled and asks for a description or a plan.
• The user says "volunteer onboarding," "shift plan," "set up a volunteer programme," or "build a
  volunteer role."
• An existing volunteer effort is losing people and needs the follow-up piece fixed.

## What you do
1. Confirm the role: what the volunteer actually does, the time commitment, and any skill or
   screening requirement. A vague role description is the first reason volunteers do not return.
2. Write the role description in plain language: what they will do, how long it takes, what they
   need to bring or know, and what impact their time has. State the impact concretely, not as a
   slogan.
3. Build the onboarding sequence: the exact steps from "said yes" to "ready for their first shift,"
   in order, each with who owns it and how long it takes.
4. Build the shift plan: what coverage is needed, by when, and how gaps get filled. Name the
   minimum number of volunteers required for the work to actually happen, not an aspirational
   number.
5. Build the recognition rhythm: a specific cadence (after every shift, monthly, at a set
   milestone) and a specific action, not a vague "we'll thank them." Treat their time as a cost the
   organisation owes something back for, not a free resource.
6. Build in the follow-up that decides whether a volunteer comes back a second time: a short check-
   in after the first shift, asked close enough to the experience to get an honest answer.

## Output
Four sections: role description, onboarding sequence (numbered steps with owners), shift plan
(coverage needed and gap-fill rule), recognition rhythm (cadence plus action). One document.

## Rules
• Never inflate the impact language beyond what the role actually does. Overstating a volunteer
  role is what makes the second shift feel like a letdown.
• State a real minimum staffing number for the shift plan, not an aspirational one.
• The follow-up after the first shift is not optional; skipping it is the most common reason
  volunteer programmes leak people.
• Ask before this material is posted publicly (a volunteer listing, a recruitment post) or sent to
  anyone outside the organisation. Drafting it is free; publishing needs a yes.
• No em dashes. Bullets use •.
