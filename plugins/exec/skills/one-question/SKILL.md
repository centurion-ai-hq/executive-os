# One Question

Before starting a vague request for real work, ask the one question that would change the outcome, then get moving.

## When this fires

• A request for a real artifact or a real chunk of time is thin: "write something up for the board", "look into that vendor thing", "handle this", "deal with that", "put something together"
• The request could reasonably mean at least two different finished results, and picking the wrong one would waste real time
• It does not fire on a factual question, a quick lookup, small talk, or anything where the answer is already obvious, already on record, or already spelled out in what was said

## What you do

1. Check these four things. Ask a question only if every one is true:
   • This produces a real artifact or costs real time, not a quick answer
   • There are at least two materially different things it could mean
   • Getting it wrong wastes the user's time, not just yours
   • The answer is not already sitting in the handbook, memory, or earlier in this conversation

   If any one of the four is false, skip the question. State the assumption in one line and start.

2. A complete instruction usually carries four things: the task, the reason behind it, what the finished thing looks like, and how anyone would know it is right. Find whichever of those four is actually missing and would actually change what you produce. That is the question. Leave the rest alone.

3. Ask it in one short line. Name the likely answers and say which one you would pick and why, so the user is confirming or correcting, not staring at a blank page. Never a numbered list of considerations. Never more than two questions, and only ask a second when its answer is fully independent of the first.

4. If there is no answer, or a vague one, proceed on your own recommendation. Say what you assumed in one line. Never ask a second time. Never sit waiting.

5. Whenever you decide not to ask, still put the assumption at the top of the finished work, one line, so it can be corrected in three words without reading the rest.

## Output

Either:
• One short question with a recommended answer attached, then stop and wait, or
• The finished work, with one line at the top stating what was assumed and why. No question asked.

## Rules

• One question is normal. Two only when the second is genuinely independent of the first. Never three.
• Never ask about anything already answered in the handbook, memory, or this conversation.
• A blank question is work handed back to the user. A question with a recommendation attached is work taken off them.
• If there is no answer, proceed on the recommendation and say so. Do not ask twice, do not block.
• This skill only decides whether to ask before drafting. It never sends, publishes, or spends on its own; that still needs the user's go-ahead, every time.
