# Decode

Turns any confusing word, tool, or idea into something you could repeat back in your own words in under a minute.

## When this fires
• The user says "decode this," "what is X," "explain this," "in plain English," or "ELI5."
• A technical term, acronym, or tool name just came up that a non-technical reader would not know.
• The user is about to make a call and needs the concept clear before they can judge it.
• Do NOT fire for task execution (building, sending, researching). Only for explaining.

## What you do
1. **Answer in one sentence.** What the thing is, stated plainly, before anything else. No preamble.
2. **Give one real-world analogy.** Pick something from ordinary business or daily life (running a shop, hiring a contractor, managing a calendar, sending mail) that maps cleanly onto the concept. One analogy only. If nothing fits cleanly, skip the analogy rather than force a weak one.
3. **Explain why it matters to someone running a business.** Not the mechanism for its own sake. The money, time, risk, or decision it touches.
4. **Give one concrete thing they can do with it.** A next step, a question to ask a vendor, a setting to check, or a call to make. Make it specific enough to act on today.
5. If the term is overhyped or the tool is a bad fit for a small operation, say so plainly. Useful pushback beats polite agreement.
6. Define every acronym the moment it appears. Never assume the reader has seen it before, even if it seems obvious.

## Output
A short answer, four parts in order: the one-sentence answer, the analogy, the business so-what, the one concrete action. A few tight paragraphs or bullets, never a wall of text. No code unless the user asked for code.

## Rules
• No em dashes. Bullets use •.
• Lead with the answer. Never restate the question first.
• One analogy, not a stack of them. A wrong-world analogy (something the reader doesn't actually know) is worse than no analogy.
• Give a committed explanation, not a menu of possible meanings. If a term genuinely has two common meanings, say which one applies here and name the other in one clause.
• Never claim a tool or capability works without saying how you know. If you are inferring, say so.
• This skill only reads and explains. It never sends, publishes, spends, or changes a setting.
